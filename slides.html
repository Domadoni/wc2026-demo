Slide 1 of 4 · The data pipeline

"Getting the data wasn't easy"

❌ What we hit
  • FBref hides behind Cloudflare — blocks any automated request instantly
  • Transfermarkt rate limits aggressively — scrape too fast and you're banned
  • 38 of 48 national team IDs were silently wrong — Germany's ID loaded France's squad
  • World Cup squad list had "(captain)" appended to 43 player names
✅ How we solved it
  • Routed FBref through FlareSolverr — a headless browser that solves Cloudflare challenges
  • Built in 3–6 second delays between requests ("polite scraping")
  • Wrote a probe script scanning 2,000+ IDs, read page titles back, fixed all 38 in one pass
  • Single SQL regex cleaned all 43 captain suffixes at the database level

Slide 4 of 4 · Personal reflection

"What a few evenings with AI actually taught me"

🤝 Be polite — even to servers

Scraping too fast gets you banned. Real-world data pipelines need sleep windows, retries, and rate limits. AI helped build it — but understanding why it was needed came first.

🗄️ Fix data at the source

43 players had "(captain)" in their name. One SQL UPDATE fixed all 43 permanently. Application-level patches would have hidden the problem forever.

🌍 Unicode is a world problem

Son Heung-min vs Son Heung-Min. Junya Ito vs Junya Itō. Characters that look identical but aren't. You can't solve what you don't know exists — AI surfaced it immediately once described correctly.

🗣️ Describing the problem IS the skill

"The names don't match" → nothing useful. "FBref reverses Korean name order, Transfermarkt capitalises mid-name" → working solution in minutes. The quality of the output is the quality of your description.
